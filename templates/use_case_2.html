Use Case 2

Grouping Retail Customers by Purchase Behavior

Problem context

A retail chain currently sends the same generic promotions to every customer, regardless of what or how often they buy. This one-size-fits-all approach means loyal customers get offers they don't need, while occasional shoppers never receive incentives that could bring them back more often. The company has years of transaction history, but has never used it to understand whether there are natural groups of customers with similar shopping habits.

Data that could be used

Purchase frequency, average ticket size, product categories purchased, discount usage, preferred shopping channel (in-store, app, or website), and time since the customer's last purchase.

What should the model discover?

Groups of customers with similar purchase patterns, such as frequent high-spenders, occasional bargain shoppers, or seasonal buyers, along with the typical shopping behavior of each group.

Type of Machine Learning that could apply

Unsupervised learning, since there are no predefined labels telling the model which customer belongs to which group. This is a clustering problem.

Potential benefit

With clear customer segments, the marketing team can design targeted campaigns, such as discounts for bargain hunters or loyalty perks for high-spenders, increasing the relevance of every promotion and improving customer satisfaction and retention.
